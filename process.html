How We Run A Project

Five phases, and exactly what each one gets you.

Risk and cost are controlled long before a shovel hits the ground. This is the sequence every Relyc project follows, and the specific outcome each phase is accountable for delivering to you.

Preconstruction & Planning

Feasibility, budget and permitting strategy.

Design Coordination

Drawings, trades and reality brought into alignment.

Estimating & Buyout

Scope, price and subcontractors locked.

Construction / Execution

Disciplined delivery on the critical path.

Closeout & Warranty

Finishing clean and standing behind the work.

  • Feasibility & site assessment
  • Conceptual estimating & budgeting
  • Constructability review
  • Value engineering
  • Permitting & entitlement strategy
  • Preliminary schedule development

Why it matters — for you

A realistic budget and schedule before you commit.

  • Coordination with owner's architect/engineer
  • Design & clash reviews
  • Scope definition & drawing packages
  • Long-lead item identification

Why it matters — for you

Fewer surprises and change orders during construction.

  • Detailed quantity takeoffs
  • Trade bid solicitation & leveling
  • Subcontractor vetting & award
  • Contract, insurance & bonding verification
  • Guaranteed pricing / GMP where applicable

Why it matters — for you

Transparent pricing and vetted trades on your job.

  • Mobilization & site logistics
  • Critical-path schedule management
  • Trade coordination & sequencing
  • Quality control program
  • Submittals, RFIs & change management
  • Cost control & pay applications
  • Regular progress reporting to the owner

Why it matters — for you

One accountable partner and clear communication throughout.

  • Punch list & final inspections
  • Certificate of Occupancy
  • As-builts, O&M manuals & warranties
  • Owner training / handover
  • Warranty & post-construction support

Why it matters — for you

A fully documented, ready-to-operate building — and support after.

Constant Across Every Phase

Four things that never move.

A

One accountable contact

The same person answers for scope, schedule and cost from the first call to the warranty period.

B

Documented decisions

Submittals, RFIs and change orders are logged and traceable — essential on government work.

C

Safety planning first

Site-specific safety planning is written into mobilization, not bolted on after an incident.

D

Regular owner reporting

Progress, cost position and schedule risk reported on a fixed cadence, not on request.
